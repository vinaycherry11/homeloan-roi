Prepay your home loan or invest the money? Worked example

By Sri Naga Vinay Mutyala · Updated November 2026

You have a ₹50 lakh home loan at 8.5% for 20 years and ₹10,000 a month to spare. Prepay the loan, or start a SIP? Prepaying closes the loan in about 12.9 years and saves about ₹21.8 L of interest. Investing comes out ahead only if your investments earn clearly more than 8.5%.

The comparison

To keep it fair, both options spend the same money every month for 20 years: EMI of ₹43,391 plus ₹10,000.

  • Prepay: pay ₹53,391 a month until the loan is gone, then invest that full amount every month until year 20.
  • Invest: pay the normal EMI and invest ₹10,000 a month for 20 years.

At year 20 both are debt-free. What differs is the size of the portfolio.

The break-even is close to the loan rate itself: if your money earns more than 8.5%, investing wins; less, and prepaying wins. Prepaying is a guaranteed 8.5% return; equity is not guaranteed.

A sensible middle path

  • Build an emergency fund first.
  • If your loan rate is high (above about 9.5%), prepaying is hard to beat on a risk-adjusted basis.
  • If it is low and you have a long horizon, investing has the edge, but split the money if the volatility would worry you.
  • When prepaying, ask the bank to reduce the tenure, not the EMI. That is what saves the most interest.

Questions people ask

Should I prepay my home loan or invest?

Compare your loan rate with the after-tax return you can reliably earn. Prepaying gives a guaranteed return equal to your loan rate (8.5% here). Investing in equity has beaten that over long periods, but with ups and downs. If the return you expect is not clearly above your loan rate, prepay.

Is prepaying a home loan a good idea?

It is a safe, guaranteed return. Prepaying ₹10,000 a month on a ₹50 lakh, 8.5% loan closes it in about 12.9 years instead of 20 and saves about ₹21.8 L of interest.

Does tax change the answer?

It can. Under the old tax regime, home-loan interest on a self-occupied home is deductible up to ₹2 lakh a year, which lowers the loan’s real cost. Equity gains are taxed when you sell. Both tilt the comparison, so check your own tax position.
