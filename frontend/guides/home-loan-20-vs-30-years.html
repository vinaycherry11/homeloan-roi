Home loan for 20 or 30 years? What the longer tenure really costs

By Sri Naga Vinay Mutyala · Updated November 2026

A longer home loan cuts your EMI but adds a lot of interest. On a ₹50 lakh loan at 8.5%, going from 20 to 30 years lowers the EMI by ₹4,945 a month and adds ₹34.3 L of interest. Whether that is a bad deal depends on what you do with the ₹4,945.

Interest grows much faster than the EMI falls: the loan is outstanding for longer, and early EMIs are mostly interest.

The fair comparison: take 30 years and invest the difference

Comparing interest alone ignores that the 30-year borrower keeps ₹4,945 a month. Suppose they invest it every month for 20 years. At the end of year 20, the 20-year borrower is debt-free; the 30-year borrower still owes ₹31 L but has a portfolio.

Investment returnPortfolio after 20 yearsLoan still owedResult at year 20
8% a year₹28.3 L₹31 L20 years ahead by ₹2.7 L
10% a year₹35.8 L₹31 L30 years + invest ahead by ₹4.8 L
12% a year₹45.5 L₹31 L30 years + invest ahead by ₹14.5 L

At equity-like returns the longer loan plus investing comes out ahead, because the portfolio grows faster than the 8.5% the loan costs. At FD-like returns it does not. Two caveats: the investing has to actually happen every month for 20 years, and equity returns can disappoint.

How to choose

  • Pick the shorter tenure if the EMI is comfortable and you would not reliably invest the difference.
  • Pick the longer tenure if a lower EMI gives you breathing room, and prepay or invest when you can. You can always pay faster; you cannot easily make a loan longer.
  • Keep an emergency fund of 6 months of EMI and expenses before choosing the shortest tenure you can afford.

Questions people ask

Is a 30-year home loan a bad idea?

Not by itself. On a ₹50 lakh loan at 8.5%, 30 years costs ₹88.4 L in interest against ₹54.1 L for 20 years, but the EMI is ₹4,945 lower. If you invest that difference every month, the longer loan can come out ahead. If you spend it, the shorter loan is cheaper.

Can I take 30 years and prepay later?

Yes. Most floating-rate home loans in India have no prepayment penalty for individuals, so a longer tenure with voluntary prepayments gives you a lower required EMI and the option to pay faster.
