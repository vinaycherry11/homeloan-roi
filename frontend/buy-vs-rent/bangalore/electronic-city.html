The 20-year comparison

The buyer pays the down payment and stamp duty on day 1, then the EMI and maintenance each month. The renter puts the same day-1 money into a Nifty 50 index fund, pays rent, and each month invests whatever the buyer spends over rent. Whoever spends less in a month invests the difference, so both start and continue with the same money.

About Electronic City

Electronic City is India's first dedicated electronics and IT industrial park, established in 1978 and spread across 800+ acres in south Bangalore. Home to Infosys and Wipro's flagship campuses along with 200+ global tech firms, it houses close to 100,000 IT employees. The Yellow Line metro (R.V. Road to Bommasandra), opened August 2025, has dramatically improved city connectivity. Residential supply is large, with a mix of affordable to mid-premium apartments across Phase 1 and 2.

  • India's first IT industrial park
  • Yellow Line metro (2025)
  • Infosys & Wipro flagship campuses
  • 200+ tech companies
  • Affordable-to-mid residential stock

Major employers nearby: Infosys (Phase 1 flagship campus), Wipro (Phase 1), TCS, HCL Technologies, Tech Mahindra, Robert Bosch.

Getting around

  • Metro: Electronic City Metro Station — Yellow Line (opened Aug 2025); Konappana Agrahara Station also on line
  • Highway: NH-44 (Bengaluru–Chennai highway) — adjacent; Elevated Expressway from Silk Board — 9.9 km
  • Peak traffic: Very heavy on Hosur Road (NH-44) and Electronic City Flyover 8–10 am and 6–9 pm; Silk Board junction is one of Bangalore's worst bottlenecks
  • Congestion: Very High

Investment view

Electronic City offers some of Bangalore's best gross rental yields, about 4–4.5%, due to large captive IT employee demand. Appreciation has been 8–10% p.a. and the 2025 Yellow Line metro opening is expected to provide a further structural uplift. Ideal for rental income investors; entry prices are lower than east Bangalore.

Questions people ask

Is it better to buy or rent in Electronic City?

Even if prices keep rising at Electronic City’s 10-year trend of 7.1% a year, renting and investing the money you would have spent on the home leaves you about ₹65.1 L richer after 20 years. Buying wins only if prices grow faster than 8.4% a year. Electronic City’s 10-year trend is 1.3 points below that. This assumes a typical 2BHK at about ₹65 L, 8.5% home loan for 20 years and a Nifty 50 index fund at 12% a year for the renter.

What is the rental yield in Electronic City?

About 4.2% gross for a typical 2BHK: roughly ₹23,000 a month on a home worth about ₹65 L. That is a price-to-rent ratio of 24, meaning the home costs 24 years of rent.

What is the EMI on a 2BHK in Electronic City?

About ₹45,127 a month on a ₹52 L loan (20% down on a ₹65 L home) at 8.5% for 20 years. In year 1, owning costs about ₹24,835 a month more than renting the same flat, after maintenance.

How fast have property prices grown in Electronic City?

About 7.1% a year on average over 2014–2024, with rents growing about 5.4% a year (Blended 10-yr trend (2014–2024) · RBI House Price Index, Anarock Research, 99acres, Knight Frank). Past growth is not a promise of future growth.
