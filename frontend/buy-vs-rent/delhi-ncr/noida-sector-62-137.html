The 20-year comparison

The buyer pays the down payment and stamp duty on day 1, then the EMI and maintenance each month. The renter puts the same day-1 money into a Nifty 50 index fund, pays rent, and each month invests whatever the buyer spends over rent. Whoever spends less in a month invests the difference, so both start and continue with the same money.

About Noida Sector 62–137

The Noida Expressway corridor — spanning Sectors 125 to 150 — is Noida's premium residential and IT address, anchored by campuses of TCS, Infosys, HCL, Wipro, and Barclays along the expressway. Sector 150 is a sporting and green township command-ing highest prices in Noida, while sectors 132 and 137 are established mid-premium IT worker catchments. Prices have risen 152% since 2019 and continue to climb on Jewar airport expectations.

Questions people ask

Is it better to buy or rent in Noida Sector 62–137?

Even if prices keep rising at Noida Sector 62–137’s 10-year trend of 6.0% a year, renting and investing the money you would have spent on the home leaves you about ₹1.55 Cr richer after 20 years. Buying wins only if prices grow faster than 8.1% a year. Noida Sector 62–137’s 10-year trend is 2.1 points below that. This assumes a typical 2BHK at about ₹1.10 Cr, 8.5% home loan for 20 years and a Nifty 50 index fund at 12% a year for the renter.

What is the rental yield in Noida Sector 62–137?

About 4.5% gross for a typical 2BHK: roughly ₹41,000 a month on a home worth about ₹1.10 Cr. That is a price-to-rent ratio of 22, meaning the home costs 22 years of rent.

What is the EMI on a 2BHK in Noida Sector 62–137?

About ₹76,368 a month on a ₹88 L loan (20% down on a ₹1.10 Cr home) at 8.5% for 20 years. In year 1, owning costs about ₹39,952 a month more than renting the same flat, after maintenance.

How fast have property prices grown in Noida Sector 62–137?

About 6.0% a year on average over 2014–2024, with rents growing about 5.5% a year (Blended 10-yr trend (2014–2024) · RBI House Price Index, Anarock Research, 99acres, Knight Frank). Past growth is not a promise of future growth.
