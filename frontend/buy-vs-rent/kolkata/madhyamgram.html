The 20-year comparison

The buyer pays the down payment and stamp duty on day 1, then the EMI and maintenance each month. The renter puts the same day-1 money into a Nifty 50 index fund, pays rent, and each month invests whatever the buyer spends over rent. Whoever spends less in a month invests the difference, so both start and continue with the same money.

About Madhyamgram

Madhyamgram is a suburb north of the airport on Jessore Road, between Birati and Barasat. It has grown with affordable apartments and independent houses, served by the Sealdah–Bangaon suburban line.

Questions people ask

Is it better to buy or rent in Madhyamgram?

Even if prices keep rising at Madhyamgram’s 10-year trend of 5.6% a year, renting and investing the money you would have spent on the home leaves you about ₹1.45 Cr richer after 20 years. Buying wins only if prices grow faster than 10.1% a year. Madhyamgram’s 10-year trend is 4.5 points below that. This assumes a typical 2BHK at about ₹40 L, 8.5% home loan for 20 years and a Nifty 50 index fund at 12% a year for the renter.

What is the rental yield in Madhyamgram?

About 3.0% gross for a typical 2BHK: roughly ₹10,000 a month on a home worth about ₹40 L. That is a price-to-rent ratio of 33, meaning the home costs 33 years of rent.

What is the EMI on a 2BHK in Madhyamgram?

About ₹27,770 a month on a ₹32 L loan (20% down on a ₹40 L home) at 8.5% for 20 years. In year 1, owning costs about ₹19,437 a month more than renting the same flat, after maintenance.

How fast have property prices grown in Madhyamgram?

About 5.6% a year on average over 2014–2024, with rents growing about 3.7% a year (Blended 10-yr trend (2014–2024) · RBI House Price Index, Anarock Research, 99acres, Knight Frank). Past growth is not a promise of future growth.
