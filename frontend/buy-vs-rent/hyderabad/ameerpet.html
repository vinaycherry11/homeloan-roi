The 20-year comparison

The buyer pays the down payment and stamp duty on day 1, then the EMI and maintenance each month. The renter puts the same day-1 money into a Nifty 50 index fund, pays rent, and each month invests whatever the buyer spends over rent. Whoever spends less in a month invests the difference, so both start and continue with the same money.

About Ameerpet

Ameerpet is Hyderabad's education and coaching hub, synonymous with software training institutes that launched thousands of IT careers in the 2000s. It is centrally located, well-connected by metro (interchange station), and has a mix of old commercial buildings and newer residential towers. Rental demand is very high from students and young professionals.

Investment view

Ameerpet delivers steady 7% appreciation and strong 4.5–5% rental yields from its captive student and young professional population. It is primarily a rental income play rather than a capital appreciation investment.

Questions people ask

Is it better to buy or rent in Ameerpet?

Even if prices keep rising at Ameerpet’s 10-year trend of 7.1% a year, renting and investing the money you would have spent on the home leaves you about ₹98.7 L richer after 20 years. Buying wins only if prices grow faster than 8.5% a year. Ameerpet’s 10-year trend is 1.4 points below that. This assumes a typical 2BHK at about ₹90 L, 8.5% home loan for 20 years and a Nifty 50 index fund at 12% a year for the renter.

What is the rental yield in Ameerpet?

About 4.4% gross for a typical 2BHK: roughly ₹33,000 a month on a home worth about ₹90 L. That is a price-to-rent ratio of 23, meaning the home costs 23 years of rent.

What is the EMI on a 2BHK in Ameerpet?

About ₹62,483 a month on a ₹72 L loan (20% down on a ₹90 L home) at 8.5% for 20 years. In year 1, owning costs about ₹33,233 a month more than renting the same flat, after maintenance.

How fast have property prices grown in Ameerpet?

About 7.1% a year on average over 2014–2024, with rents growing about 4.6% a year (Blended 10-yr trend (2014–2024) · RBI House Price Index, Anarock Research, 99acres, Knight Frank). Past growth is not a promise of future growth.
