Method

  • Typical 2BHK price: the middle of each locality’s price-per-sq-ft range times a typical 2BHK size for the city (Hyderabad 1,200 sq ft, Bangalore 1,150 sq ft, Pune 1,000 sq ft, Mumbai 750 sq ft, Delhi NCR 1,300 sq ft, Chennai 1,050 sq ft, Kolkata 1,000 sq ft, Ahmedabad 1,150 sq ft).
  • Rent: the 2BHK rent from our locality research where we have one, otherwise the price times the locality’s gross yield, capped at 4.5%.
  • Growth: each locality’s 2014–2024 price and rent growth, blended from RBI House Price Index, Anarock, Knight Frank and 99acres data.
  • Model: Typical 2BHK prices and rents are our estimates for 2026, not listings. Loan at 8.5% for 20 years with 20% down; 7% stamp duty + registration (actual rates vary by state); maintenance 0.5% of the home value a year; the renter invests in a Nifty 50 index fund at 12% a year. Price and rent growth follow each locality’s 2014–2024 trend, which may not continue. Figures are before tax and not adjusted for inflation.

Questions people ask

Is it better to buy or rent a house in India?

In our 2026 model of a typical 2BHK, buying comes out ahead in 19 of 168 localities across 8 cities if prices keep rising at their 10-year trends. Buying wins mainly in fast-growing IT corridors; in expensive, slower-growing markets like most of Mumbai and South Delhi, renting and investing the difference wins.

What price growth does buying need to beat renting?

With a 8.5% loan and a Nifty 50 fund at 12%, half our localities need prices to grow between 8.8% and 9.8% a year for buying to win, because rental yields of 3–4.5% are well below the loan rate.

What is a good price-to-rent ratio in India?

Price-to-rent ratio is the home price divided by a year’s rent. Our localities range from about 22 to 37. The lower it is, the more the rent you save pays back the price, and the less price growth buying needs. See our price-to-rent guide.
